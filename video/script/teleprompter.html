Recording the voice for the Stability video

  • What to record: sound only. Voice Memos on a phone is fine, or QuickTime, File, New Audio Recording.
  • Where: a quiet room with soft things in it (a bedroom beats a kitchen). Hold the phone about a hand's length from your mouth.
  • How: one recording, start to finish. Press the right arrow or space for the next card and pause two seconds between cards.
  • Messed up a line? Pause, then say that whole card again from its first word and carry on. The last clean take of each card is the one that gets used.
  • Pace: calm and clear, a little slower than talking to a friend. The 313 words should take about 1 minute 45 seconds. The limit is 2 minutes.
  • Two voices? You can take turns card by card. Keep it one file, or one file each, in card order.
  • Then: put the file in video/takes/ and run python3 tools/ingest_audio.py takes/your-file.m4a, then render again. The pictures re-time themselves to your voice.

Every month, it asks the two questions doctors use. Maya says she's mostly okay, and it takes her at her word. Then finals hit. Her check-ins get tense, and our model doesn't wait a month. It asks again.

But at week eight, her answers come back high. Maya sees a nudge to see a doctor, and her mom gets a note: what changed, for how long, and how to start the conversation. Never her messages.

Under the hood, her digital life, Stable's two taps and her past answers feed our engine. It tracks how she's doing and decides when to ask. High answers unlock the full eight questions, and only then is a parent told.

8 / 9It works

And it works. Tested on over four hundred thousand real examples, our ask model finds a hard week three times as often as a schedule, and scored point nine oh on a hundred thirteen teens it had never seen. Across thirty thousand US high schoolers, those sleeping four hours or less were twice as likely to feel sad or hopeless. Our language models catch twice what keyword lists do.
