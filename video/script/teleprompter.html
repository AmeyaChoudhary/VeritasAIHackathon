Recording the voice for the Stability video

  • What to record: sound only. Voice Memos on a phone is fine, or QuickTime, File, New Audio Recording.
  • Where: a quiet room with soft things in it (a bedroom beats a kitchen). Hold the phone about a hand's length from your mouth.
  • How: one recording, start to finish. Press the right arrow or space for the next card and pause two seconds between cards.
  • Messed up a line? Pause, then say that whole card again from its first word and carry on. The last clean take of each card is the one that gets used.
  • Pace: calm and clear, a little slower than talking to a friend. The 369 words should take about 1 minute 45 seconds. The limit is 2 minutes.
  • Two voices? You can take turns card by card. Keep it one file, or one file each, in card order.
  • Then: put the file in video/takes/ and run python3 tools/ingest_audio.py takes/your-file.m4a, then render again. The pictures re-time themselves to your voice.

Maya is sixteen. Last fall she quit soccer. By December she slept five hours a night and texted her friends half as much. Her mom thought it was finals. Two months passed before anyone asked how she really was. Maya is one of many: one in five US teens has depression each year, and fifty-nine percent get no treatment. Nobody knows when to ask.

3 / 9Her digital life, with consent

With consent from Maya and her parents, it connects to her digital life: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, browsing, news feeds, Spotify, games and chats. It's all read on her phone, and her parents never see a message, search or video. Once a week, our mascot Stable checks in: two taps.

4 / 9It asks, and asks early

Every month, Stable asks the two screening questions doctors use. In week four, Maya scores two of six and is taken at her word. During finals, her stress check-ins jump from two to four, our ask model's score crosses its line, and it asks again two weeks early.

On screen: Week 4: the two questions; she answers 'several days' to both, 2 of 6. Weeks 5 and 6: the ring turns amber, her check-in stress goes from 2 to 4, the ask model's score climbs from 0.05 past 0.16, and the questions come up again in week 6.

6 / 9Private, with a safety net

Small models read what she types, watches and searches, keep only numbers and delete the words. If she types about self-harm, 988 is one tap away.

On screen: Maya types a message. It becomes a single dot. A counter holds at 0 bytes sent. A chip: 988, one tap away.
7 / 9The engine

Under the hood, her two taps and past answers feed our ask model, a logistic regression scoring the chance her next screening is high. Past sixteen percent, it asks. Three or more unlocks all eight; ten or more alerts a parent.

On screen: A diagram builds: two taps and past answers feed the ask model, a logistic regression; past 16% it asks; 2 questions, then all 8 at 3 or more, then a note at 10 or more. Below: her digital life feeds the mood, self-harm and music models, all on the phone.
8 / 9It works

Does it work? We tested on over four hundred thousand labelled examples: check-ins, surveys and real posts. Our ask model finds a hard week three times as often as a fixed schedule. On a hundred thirteen teens it had never seen, its AUC was point nine oh. In a CDC survey, teens sleeping four hours or less were twice as likely to feel hopeless.
