Recording the voice for the Stability video

  • What to record: sound only. Voice Memos on a phone is fine, or QuickTime, File, New Audio Recording.
  • Where: a quiet room with soft things in it (a bedroom beats a kitchen). Hold the phone about a hand's length from your mouth.
  • How: one recording, start to finish. Press the right arrow or space for the next card and pause two seconds between cards.
  • Messed up a line? Pause, then say that whole card again from its first word and carry on. The last clean take of each card is the one that gets used.
  • Pace: calm and clear, a little slower than talking to a friend. The 319 words should take about 1 minute 45 seconds. The limit is 2 minutes.
  • Two voices? You can take turns card by card. Keep it one file, or one file each, in card order.
  • Then: put the file in video/takes/ and run python3 tools/ingest_audio.py takes/your-file.m4a, then render again. The pictures re-time themselves to your voice.

That's why we built Stability. It lives on a teenager's phone, linked to a parent's. Meet Maya.

3 / 9It learns her normal

For four weeks, Stability learns her normal: sleep, reaching out, movement, and the tone of what she types. Once a week, our mascot, Stable, checks in with her. Two taps.

On screen: Maya's phone in the centre. The ring fills over four weeks. The four signals light up as they are named. Then Stable, the mascot, floats in, drifts around, and settles into the check-in card on her phone.
4 / 9It asks

Every month, it asks two questions, the ones doctors use. Maya says she's mostly okay, and it takes her at her word. Then finals hit. Her check-ins get tense, and our model doesn't wait a month. It asks again.

On screen: Week four: the two questions come up and she answers low. Weeks five and six: the ring turns amber, her check-ins climb, the model's score crosses its line, and the questions come up again.
5 / 9The note

But at week eight, her answers come back high. Maya sees a nudge to see a doctor, and her mom gets a note: what changed, for how long, and how to start the conversation. Never her messages.

On screen: Week six she answers low again. Week eight her answers are high. Mom's phone slides in. An amber dot crosses from Maya's phone to hers and the note opens.
6 / 9Private, with a safety net

What Maya types never leaves her phone. A small model reads each message there, keeps one dot, and lets the words go. And if she types words about self-harm, 988 is one tap away.

On screen: Maya types a message. It becomes a bubble, then a single dot. A counter holds at 0 bytes sent. A chip: 988, one tap away.
7 / 9The engine

Under the hood, Stable's two taps and her past answers feed our ask model, which decides when to ask. High answers unlock the full eight questions, and only then is a parent told. Two on-device language models read mood and self-harm risk, and no words ever leave the phone.

On screen: A diagram builds: two taps and past answers feed the ask model; it opens the two questions, then the eight, then the note. Below, typed words feed two on-phone models. A lock: 0 words leave the phone.
8 / 9It works

And it works. Tested on over three hundred fifty thousand real examples, our ask model finds a hard week over three times as often as a schedule. On a hundred thirteen teenagers it had never seen, it scored an AUC of point nine oh, and nine in ten of its top asks found a struggling teen. Our language models catch twice what keyword lists do.
